There is a kind of writing that does not fit neatly into a paper. A paper is
built to defend a claim. A journal can hold the parts that come before the
claim: the half-formed question, the reading note, the story from a long week,
the idea that might become a project.

That is what this space is for.

## What you will find here

The journal is organized into four loose categories:

- **Thoughts** for essays and opinions that are still taking shape.
- **Stories** for narratives and observations with a beginning and an end.
- **Research Notes** for technical working notes, reading summaries, and
  experiments that may never become publications.
- **Life** for everything outside the lab.

Categories are deliberately broad. A piece can start as a research note and
turn into a story; the archive is more useful when it does not force a decision
too early.

## A slower format

Most writing on the internet is optimized for the first ten seconds. This one
is optimized for the person who arrives with twenty minutes and wants to think.

That means a few small commitments:

1. Write in full sentences.
2. Show the reasoning, not only the conclusion.
3. Link to sources when they exist.
4. Leave placeholders empty rather than filling them with guesses.

The last point matters on a personal website. It is tempting to make a page
look complete by inventing a date, a metric, or an award. The better habit is
to mark what is missing until it is true.
