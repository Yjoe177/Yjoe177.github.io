Some feelings can't be described. Sometimes, when you're trying to explain what you're feeling, you just end up saying, "I don't know, I don't know... it's like... mmm, I don't know."

I've always found myself trying to understand what I'm feeling. Am I happy? Sad? Confused? Am I feeling good or bad? I thought that if I could name my emotions, I would understand myself better, recognize how things affect me, and discover more about who I am.

But lately, I've started questioning something: Why are we always chasing a description of our feelings? Why do we feel the need to give everything we feel a name?

Maybe it's because naming our emotions gives us a sense of understanding, and understanding gives us a sense of control. When we say, "I'm sad," "I'm anxious," or "I'm happy," we turn something invisible and complicated into something we can recognize.

But not every feeling needs to be understood to be real.

Sometimes what we feel isn't exactly sadness, happiness, or confusion. It's somewhere in between, or maybe something entirely different. And the more we try to describe it, the further we get from actually experiencing it.

This made me think about something interesting in Arabic.

The Arabic word for heart is "Qalb" (قلب), which comes from a root meaning "to turn" or "to change."

It is said that the heart was given this name because it constantly changes from one state to another, in its feelings, thoughts, beliefs, and conditions.

The heart has even been compared to a feather being turned by the winds in the desert, or a pot of boiling water, constantly moving and unsettled.

I find this fascinating because even in its name, the heart carries the meaning of change. It was never meant to remain in one state. Yet we keep trying to give fixed names to something whose nature is to change.

Maybe knowing ourselves doesn't always mean being able to explain everything happening inside us. Maybe it also means accepting that some things cannot immediately be put into words.

And perhaps there's a difference between understanding our feelings and constantly analyzing them. Sometimes our curiosity brings us closer to ourselves, but other times it makes us overthink something that simply needs to be felt.

Maybe the heart was never meant to be fully explained, but to be experienced. We call it Qalb because it changes, yet we keep asking it to give us a fixed answer.

And this leaves me wondering:

When I can't describe what I'm feeling, does that make me uncomfortable because I don't understand myself? Or can I actually enjoy the mystery of not knowing?

Maybe sometimes, "I don't know what I'm feeling" is enough.

And maybe that's part of knowing myself, too.
